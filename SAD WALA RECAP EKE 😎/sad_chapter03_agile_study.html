Introduction to Agile

Topic 1
Why Agile?

Problems Agile Solved

Traditional development models faced these challenges:

  • Late delivery of software
  • Difficulty handling changing requirements
  • Limited customer involvement throughout
💡 Agile was introduced specifically to overcome these limitations of Waterfall-style development.
Definition

What is Agile?

A set of principles and practices for software development that emphasises:

  • Flexibility and adaptability
  • Customer collaboration throughout
  • Frequent, early delivery of working software
  • Continuous feedback and incremental improvement
⚡ Key: Agile is adaptive, not predictive. Software developed in small increments called iterations or sprints.
Important Clarification

Agile Does NOT Eliminate Planning

A common misconception is that Agile means "no planning." In reality:

  • Planning is done continuously throughout the project
  • Plans adapt as new information emerges
  • Each sprint has its own planning session
  • The product backlog is continuously refined
Agile Promotes

Core Agile Focus Areas

  • 🤝 Teamwork — cross-functional, self-organising teams
  • 🔄 Continuous feedback — from users and stakeholders
  • 📈 Incremental improvement — each sprint improves on the last
  • 💬 Face-to-face communication — preferred over written
  • ⚡ Early delivery — working software from sprint 1

The Agile Manifesto — 4 Core Values

Topic 2 — Very High Exam Weight
1

Individuals & Interactions

✓ over Processes and Tools
over Processes and Tools

People and communication matter more than rigid processes or specific tools. A great team with basic tools beats a poor team with the best tools.

2

Working Software

✓ over Comprehensive Documentation

Delivering functional software is the primary goal. Documentation is valuable but should not slow down delivery of working features.

3

Customer Collaboration

✓ over Contract Negotiation

Ongoing partnership with the customer produces better outcomes than rigid, contract-driven relationships where change is penalised.

4

Responding to Change

✓ over Following a Plan

Adapting to new requirements and circumstances is valued over sticking to an outdated plan. Change is expected and welcomed.

⚠️ Critical Note

The "Over" Wording Matters

The Agile Manifesto says "while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more." Agile does NOT say documentation, tools, contracts, and plans are worthless — it says the left-side items are prioritised over the right-side items. This distinction is frequently tested.

Key Characteristics of Agile Development

Topic 4
Characteristic

🔁 Iterative & Incremental

Development done in repeated cycles, each adding to and refining the previous version of the system.

Characteristic

⏱️ Short Development Cycles

Sprints last 2–4 weeks. Each sprint produces a potentially shippable product increment.

Characteristic

👥 Continuous User Involvement

Users and stakeholders are engaged throughout — not just at requirements and delivery.

Characteristic

📄 Minimal Documentation

Minimal but sufficient documentation — enough to guide work, not enough to slow it down.

Characteristic

🧪 Frequent Testing

Testing and integration happen continuously — not just at the end of the project.

Characteristic

🔀 High Adaptability

Teams can change direction quickly in response to evolving requirements or market feedback.

Characteristic

🏗️ Cross-Functional Teams

Teams include all skills needed to complete a sprint — developers, testers, designers, analysts — without external dependencies.

Common Agile Methods

Topic 5
Most Popular

🏉 Scrum

The most widely used Agile framework. Uses sprints, defined roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team), events, and artifacts. Ideal for complex product development.

Method

⚡ Extreme Programming (XP)

Focuses on technical excellence — pair programming, test-driven development (TDD), continuous integration, and refactoring. Highly disciplined coding practices.

Method

📋 Kanban

Visual workflow management using a board with columns (To Do / In Progress / Done). Focuses on limiting work in progress (WIP) to improve flow and reduce bottlenecks.

Method

💎 Crystal

A family of Agile methodologies scaled to team size and project criticality. Crystal Clear, Crystal Yellow, Crystal Orange for different team sizes.

Method

🎯 Feature-Driven Development (FDD)

Organizes development around building and delivering features (client-valued functions). Uses a feature list and model-driven approach. Good for larger teams.

Scrum Framework — Roles

Topic 6 — Core Exam Topic
👔
Role 1

Product Owner

  • Represents customer and stakeholders
  • Responsible for maximizing product value
  • Manages and owns the Product Backlog
  • Prioritizes requirements based on business value
  • Decides what gets built and in what order
🛡️
Role 2

Scrum Master

  • Facilitates the Scrum process
  • Removes obstacles (impediments)
  • Ensures Scrum rules are followed
  • Acts as a servant leader — serves the team
  • Protects the team from outside interference
💻
Role 3

Development Team

  • Cross-functional professionals
  • Responsible for delivering the product increment
  • Self-organising — decides how to do the work
  • Typically 3–9 members
  • No sub-teams or hierarchies within

Scrum Framework — Events

Topic 7
🏃

Sprint

The core of Scrum — a fixed-duration development cycle (usually 2–4 weeks). No changes allowed once a sprint starts. Goal: deliver a working, potentially releasable increment.

Duration: 2–4 weeks | Produces: Working Increment
📋

Sprint Planning

Held at the start of each sprint. The team decides what to build in the sprint by selecting items from the Product Backlog. Sets the Sprint Goal.

When: Start of sprint | Output: Sprint Backlog
☀️

Daily Scrum (Daily Standup)

A 15-minute daily meeting for the development team. Three questions: What was done yesterday? What will be done today? Any impediments?

Duration: 15 minutes | Frequency: Every day | For: Dev team
🎯

Sprint Review

Held at the end of the sprint. The team demonstrates completed work to stakeholders and collects feedback. Product Backlog may be updated based on feedback.

When: End of sprint | Output: Feedback, updated backlog
🔍

Sprint Retrospective

The team reflects on the process (not the product). Identifies what went well, what didn't, and what to improve in the next sprint.

When: After Sprint Review | Focus: Process improvement

Scrum Framework — Artifacts

Topic 8

📦 Product Backlog

  • Ordered list of all requirements for the product
  • Continuously refined and re-prioritized
  • Owned and managed by the Product Owner
  • Single source of truth for what needs to be done
  • Never complete — evolves as the product evolves

📝 Sprint Backlog

  • A subset of the Product Backlog
  • Selected items to be completed in the current sprint
  • Created during Sprint Planning
  • Owned by the Development Team
  • Cannot be changed once sprint starts

✅ Increment

  • Sum of all completed backlog items from current and previous sprints
  • Must be usable and potentially releasable
  • Must meet the team's "Definition of Done"
  • Demonstrates real progress to stakeholders

Advantages & Limitations of Agile

Topic 9

✅ Advantages

  • Faster delivery of working software
  • Better customer satisfaction
  • Early detection of issues and defects
  • Improved team collaboration
  • Adaptable to changing requirements
  • Continuous feedback reduces wrong-system risk

❌ Limitations

  • Less suitable for very large teams
  • Requires active customer involvement
  • Difficult to estimate cost and schedule initially
  • Depends heavily on team skills and maturity
  • Minimal documentation can cause issues later
  • Scope creep risk if backlog not managed well
